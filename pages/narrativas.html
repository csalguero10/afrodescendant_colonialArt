COLLECTION • THEMES

Which theme
speaks to you?

Casta paintings, produced mainly in New Spain during the eighteenth century, depict in series of canvases the unions between Spaniards, Indigenous people, and Africans, and the children born of them, identified with labels such as mulato, morisco, or lobo. Often commissioned to be sent to Europe, these works sought to display the wealth and diversity of the Americas, but also to order them. They always placed the Spaniard at the top and associated mixture with African ancestry with a blood that, in the thinking of the time, could not be “cleansed.” Women of African descent often appear as proud, hot-tempered, or violent, a stereotype that coexisted with the admiration and scandal their skirts and jewelry provoked. These images are not portraits of reality but a visual instrument that naturalized colonial hierarchy. Reading them critically makes it possible to separate the order they project from a society far more fluid and conflicted than the one they sought to fix.
