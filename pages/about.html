ABOUT THE EXHIBITION

This virtual exhibition complements the physical exhibit and seeks to challenge the oversimplified idea that all Afro-descendant people in the colonial era were enslaved.

Through secular works, religious paintings, and portraits, we will discover how Afro-descendant people held diverse and complex roles in colonial society: merchants, artisans, religious leaders, and trusted figures within the elites.

This exhibition seeks to reveal the complexity of representations of Afro-descendant people in colonial art, showing roles that go beyond slavery: merchants, artisans, religious leaders, and trusted figures within the colonial elites.
During the Latin American colonial period (16th–19th centuries), Afro-descendant people were a fundamental part of the social fabric. While many arrived as enslaved people, others were free individuals who practiced specialized trades and held positions of responsibility across different social strata.
These paintings are more than historical records: they are essential clues for better understanding the sociocultural dynamics that still shape our contemporary society, helping us question stereotypes and recognize the historical diversity of Afro-descendant communities.
The works were selected based on criteria of historical representativeness, diversity of contexts, and clarity in depicting Afro-descendant people in diverse roles. Each piece was analyzed considering elements such as clothing, spatial position, gestures, and social context — applying Panofsky's iconographic method to the ARCA archive corpus.
