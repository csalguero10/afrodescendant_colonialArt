01 / CONTEXT

PROJECT CONTEXT

Presencias is a virtual exhibition that complements the physical exhibit and stems from doctoral research that computationally applies Erwin Panofsky's iconographic method to the study of representations of Afro-descendant people in Latin American colonial art (16th–19th centuries).

The project sits at the intersection of Art History, Digital Humanities, and Computer Vision. It builds on the corpus of the ARCA archive (Universidad de los Andes, Bogotá) and incorporates visual analysis techniques assisted by vision-language models (VLMs) for iconographic annotation at scale.

"The goal is not to replace the art historian's eye, but to amplify their ability to detect patterns in corpora of thousands of images that would otherwise be unmanageable."

Excerpt from the research project, 2025–2026

Project Timeline

2024 — Phase 1

Corpus Construction

Scraping and cleaning the ARCA archive with Playwright and Python. Downloaded ~3,400 high-resolution images with Dublin Core metadata.

2025 — Phase 2

Assisted Iconographic Annotation

ColonialArt-VLM pipeline: using GPT-4V and Claude to annotate clothing, spatial position, gestures, and objects. Manual validation by colonial art specialists.

2025–2026 — Phase 3

Exhibition & Outreach

Design of the physical exhibit in Bogotá and of this virtual exhibition. Publication of the annotated data under a CC-BY license.

02 / METHODOLOGY

METHODOLOGY

The research applies Panofsky's three-level model — pre-iconographic description, iconographic analysis, and iconological interpretation — as the conceptual framework for designing the computer vision pipeline.

Each level corresponds to a feature-extraction layer: detection of objects and figures (level 1), classification of symbolic attributes (level 2), and inference of social roles and power relations (level 3).

01

Corpus Selection and Scraping

Automated extraction from the ARCA archive with Playwright. Cleaning and normalization of metadata according to Dublin Core. Storage in Google Sheets via gspread.

02

VLM Annotation

Structured prompting of vision-language models. The model identifies clothing, accessories, position, gestures, and spatial context. The response is stored as normalized JSON.

03

Iconographic Validation

Manual review of 15% of the corpus by specialists. Calculation of inter-annotator agreement metrics (Cohen's κ). Correction of systematic model errors.

04

Curation and Exhibition

Selection of representative works for the exhibit. Writing curatorial entries at two levels of depth: general public and specialists. Design of the virtual exhibition with SvelteKit + static HTML.
