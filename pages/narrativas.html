FACTS THAT TRANSFORM

40%

Of the enslaved people who arrived in the Americas during the colonial period gained their freedom during their lifetime, many through manumission or specialized work.

16

Distinct casta categories documented in 18th-century paintings, revealing the complexity of the colonial racial classification system.

17th c.

Century of greatest casta painting production in New Spain, coinciding with the rise of the Bourbon reforms and the Enlightenment's taxonomic drive.

Casta paintings are a pictorial genre unique to the Viceroyalty of New Spain (Mexico) and the Viceroyalty of Peru, produced mainly in the 18th century. They document the 16 racial mixtures recognized by the colonial system, depicting couples of different ethnic backgrounds together with their children.
Free Afro-descendant people in the colonial era worked as artisans, coachmen, street vendors, musicians, and even as militia members. In Cuba, Jamaica, and Peru, there were communities of freedmen who owned property and businesses of their own.
In colonial paintings, clothing is a social document: embroidery, fabric types, and accessories indicate the status of the person portrayed. An Afro-descendant coachman wearing a European redingote signals his integration into urban life and his role as a trusted servant of a wealthy family.
